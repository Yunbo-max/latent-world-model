# R12 v3 source audit — Gram-component autonomous closure

## Scope and claim discipline

This audit supports only source-facing classification of `R12_GRAM_COMPONENT_CLOSURE.v3.md`. The finite-family graph theorem is a derivation specialized to symmetric rank-one Delta factors. It is **not** presented as the first invariant-subspace theorem, the first exact abstraction, or a novel architecture.

## Primary/authoritative anchors already fixed in this packet

1. **DeltaNet / Delta-rule memory.** The DeltaNet paper and author materials define the rank-one Delta update family used as the object of study. R12 does not alter its basic write rule; it studies exact reduced-state closure under a declared family of its left factors.
2. **Linear-system bisimulation and exact abstraction.** George J. Pappas, *Bisimilar Linear Systems* (Automatica 39(12), 2003; author-hosted PDF), provides the decisive general collision class: exact quotient/state abstractions are governed by invariant-kernel/subspace conditions. R12 v3 is a narrow rank-one symmetric corollary/construction, not a replacement for that theory.
3. **Internal R18 collision record.** The packet's earlier exact recursive quotient/bisimulation work already establishes the general closure lens. R12 v3 may contribute a convenient finite-key graph certificate, but it cannot be counted as an independent general quotient mechanism.

## Novelty search outcome

Targeted searches around simultaneous rank-one invariant/reducing subspaces, non-orthogonality graphs, and block decomposition did not establish a reliable priority claim for this exact graph phrasing. The construction is short enough to be viewed as an immediate corollary of elementary invariant-subspace algebra. Absence of a retrieved identical phrase is not evidence of originality.

Accordingly the only defensible label is:

> Delta-specific finite-family characterization/control, mathematically useful if correct; originality and independent method status not established.

## Nearby mechanisms and controls

- Simultaneous block diagonalization and common-invariant-subspace work is close at the representation level.
- Orthogonal block memories, slots, sparse routing and protected/orthogonal updates are close engineering controls when the key family is structured.
- Full Delta/WY and direct reduced coordinates with the same `U` are decisive same-information baselines.
- Thresholded Gram graphs fall back to approximate projection/truncation and require their own accumulated-error analysis.

## Author-code interface boundary

The source audit does not claim that an author implementation exposes a finite admissible key dictionary, a Gram-component certificate, or an online closure API. Ordinary Delta implementations generate keys from current hidden states; those keys are not generally a fixed family declared before compression. Therefore the v3 theorem is not silently attributed to existing code, and its key-family assumption must be charged as external structure.

## Measurement-source boundary

Existing native language-task assets score endpoint predictions. They do not natively expose internal key dictionaries, graph components, exact closure rank, or interventions isolating the carrier. The result can be checked on a synthetic/external linear fixture, but no such fixture is promoted to an empirical benchmark or reported result in this stage.

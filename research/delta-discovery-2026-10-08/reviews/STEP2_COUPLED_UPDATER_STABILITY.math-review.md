## Necessary correction

Section 5 correctly proves that a fixed nonzero linear protected readout `Lx` cannot preserve an initial distinction under uniform full-state contraction on the same external-input path. However, its broader wording says that differences of any fixed *continuous* readout must vanish whenever state distances vanish. Continuity alone does not imply this on an unbounded reachable set.

An analytic counterexample uses `gamma=1/2` and time-dependent scalar maps `T_t(x)=gamma*x+(t+1-gamma*t)`. These maps uniformly contract differences. Starting at `x_0=0` and `x'_0=delta>0` gives `x_t=t`, `x'_t=t+2^{-t}delta`. The fixed continuous readout `f(x)=exp(2x)` has difference `exp(2t)[exp(2^{1-t}delta)-1]`, bounded below by `2delta*(exp(2)/2)^t`, hence diverging. No numerical execution is needed.

Replace the nonlinear-readout extension by “fixed bounded linear readout L” (the document's actual object), or require uniform continuity on the reachable domain, e.g. compactness or a uniform Lipschitz bound. The exact-invariant linear-L argument itself needs no change: `||L delta x_t|| <= ||L|| gamma^t ||delta h_0||` tends to zero while the alleged preserved difference remains nonzero.
